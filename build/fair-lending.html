Fair Lending Compliance with BiasOps

How a mid-size bank goes from deploying a credit scoring model to producing a full regulatory audit in under 4 minutes, with policies embedded in the pipeline from the start.

Real-world scenario

Acme Bank · AI Credit Scoring Audit

Acme Bank deploys an XGBoost credit scoring model trained on 5 years of historical lending data. The model processes 10,000+ loan applications per quarter. Regulators want proof it does not discriminate across race, gender, and age, and that denial reasons are specific, not generic. Today that audit takes 6 weeks and $200K in consulting fees. With BiasOps, it takes 4 minutes.

Why fair lending is the highest-stakes AI compliance problem

The AI lending market is projected to reach $2 trillion by 2037. Every bank and fintech deploying ML in credit decisions faces an overlapping web of federal regulations: ECOA, HMDA, CFPB guidance, OCC risk management, and the Fair Housing Act. The CFPB has made it clear: creditors cannot provide generic denial reasons for AI-driven decisions. They must give specific, individualized explanations.

$89 million in US penalties have already been levied for AI lending discrimination. The question is not whether your model has bias. The question is whether you can prove it does not, on demand, with an audit trail regulators will accept.

BiasOps does not bolt compliance on after deployment. It embeds policy checks directly into the ML pipeline, so every model prediction is validated against real regulatory thresholds before it reaches a customer.

5 policies that ship with the fair lending pack

Each policy maps directly to a published federal regulation. They're defined in YAML, version-controlled in Git, and enforced automatically in your pipeline.

Critical

ECOA Disparate Impact

ecoa-disparate-impact.yaml · Equal Credit Opportunity Act / Reg B
Disparate impact ratio ≥ 0.80 (4/5ths rule) across race, sex, age, national origin
Selection rate comparison across all protected classes
Less discriminatory alternative (LDA) documentation required
Critical

HMDA Reporting Compliance

hmda-reporting-compliance.yaml · Home Mortgage Disclosure Act / Reg C
HMDA data fields completeness (action taken, loan purpose, race, sex, income)
Denial rate disparity across demographic groups
Geographic lending pattern analysis for redlining detection
Critical

CFPB Adverse Action Notices

cfpb-adverse-action.yaml · CFPB Circular 2023-03
Model must produce specific, individualized denial reasons, not generic buckets
Adverse action reasons must map to actual model features
Reason code specificity score ≥ 0.70
High

SR 11-7 Model Risk Management

sr11-7-model-risk.yaml · OCC/Fed SR 11-7
Model documentation completeness (model card, validation report, limitations)
Independent validation evidence required
Ongoing monitoring plan and change management controls documented
Critical

Fair Housing Act (Credit)

fair-housing-act-credit.yaml · Fair Housing Act
Protected class proxy variable detection (ZIP code → race proxy)
Redlining pattern detection in geographic lending data
Feature importance audit for prohibited correlations

What a policy looks like in code

This is the ECOA disparate impact policy, the centerpiece check in the fair lending pack. It lives in your repo, gets reviewed in pull requests, and enforces automatically on every deployment.

policies/financial-services/ecoa-disparate-impact.yaml 
- check_id: "ecoa_adverse_impact_ratio"
  name: "ECOA Adverse Impact Ratio"
  regulation: "ECOA / Reg B"
  metric: "adverse_impact_ratio"
  threshold: 0.80
  operator: ">="
  protected_attributes:
    - "race"
    - "sex"
    - "age"
    - "national_origin"
  severity: "critical"
  remediation: "Review model features for disparate impact. Document less discriminatory alternatives."
  evidence_required:
    - "selection_rate_by_group"
    - "feature_importance_audit"
    - "lda_analysis"
